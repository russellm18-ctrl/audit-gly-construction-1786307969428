## Atmospheric Reality Gap

The hero image attached shows a desaturated, gray-toned concrete pour shot with GLY's yellow logo overlaid at center mass. The desaturation drains scale and energy from what is objectively a dramatic scene — a multi-crane, multi-crew foundation pour in a dense urban core. That's a $100M+ project atmosphere reduced to a background texture. The logo overlay compounds the damage: it sits directly over the most compositionally active zone of the frame, obscuring the workers, the rebar grid, and the crane geometry that would signal complexity and capacity to an owner's rep evaluating general contractors. What a high-value buyer sees is wallpaper, not proof.

The copy promises "constructive optimism," seeing around corners, and solving problems before they exist. The hero delivers institutional gray. That gap is not aesthetic — it's a trust gap. A developer comparing two GCs and landing on this page reads caution where the copy promises confidence.

Supporting this: Image 3's alt text — "Landscape photo of moon-lit city with lake in foreground and mountains in background" — confirms at least one content image is a regional beauty shot, not a project demonstration. Scenery signals love of place. It does not signal that GLY built anything in it.

The trust cost: buyers who can't see capability in the first eight seconds don't call. They move to the next tab.

## Visual Narrative Coherence

The homepage link audit is clean — 25 links checked, 0 broken. That structural discipline is real. But the visual journey from hero to project proof fractures before it earns the click. The "Groundbreakers. Market Makers." section leads with copy that promises to show the region's most innovative builds, then drops Image 2 and Image 4 with no alt text and blob URLs — meaning those images rendered in scrape as unidentified assets with zero context. A buyer who reads "market makers" and lands on an unnamed image has just been handed a broken promise in visual form.

The Microsoft Thermal Energy Center callout — an ENR Best of the Best recognition — is the strongest proof point on the page, and it's buried below the careers pitch. An award from ENR national carries immediate vendor-qualification weight with facility managers and real estate developers. It is not the lead. That inversion tells a high-value buyer that GLY doesn't yet know which evidence closes deals.

## Metadata and Conversion Gaps

Five of eight content images carry no alt text — 63% coverage failure. In a sector where project search terms like "Puget Sound commercial general contractor" and "Pacific Northwest data center construction" drive owner-side research, every untagged image is invisible to image search and weakens topical relevance signals for the pages those images anchor.

The meta description is missing entirely. Google is auto-generating one from page copy, which means GLY has surrendered control of the first sentence a prospect reads before clicking. At #1 organic rank for "GLY Construction," that's a branded search you already own — and you're letting an algorithm write your value proposition into the SERP.

The page title at 36 characters — "Puget Sound General Contractor | GLY" — is functional but generic. It does not reference project type, scale, or market specialty. Any GC in the region could run that title. Combined with the missing meta description, the search snippet for a $500M-revenue contractor reads like a placeholder.

The og:image is present, which protects LinkedIn and email share appearance — that's the one infrastructure win here. Everything else in the findability stack is leaving qualified traffic to self-sort.
